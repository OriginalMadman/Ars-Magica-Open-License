# Aldric of Mercere — Combat Playbook

What to cast, what to swing at, and in what order — with every number derived from the rule
that produces it. Assumes the sheet in `character-sheet.md`.

---

## Fight card

Everything he needs mid-fight, on one screen. **Field conditions: in full harness (Encumbrance
2), no aura.**

| Roll | Total | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiative, sword** | **+1 + stress die** | Qik +1 + sword Init +2 + shield 0 − Encumbrance 2 |
| **Initiative, casting** | **+2 + stress die** | Qik +1 − Encumbrance 2 + Fast Caster 3. *Spells take no weapon Initiative modifier* |
| **Attack** | **+13 + stress die** | Dex +2 + Single Weapon 7 + sword Atk +4 + shield Atk 0 |

## The action economy — what one round buys

> "It takes approximately one combat round to cast a spell, unless it is fast-cast. Thus, a magus
> cannot cast more than one normal spell in a single round, but may also cast a fast-cast spell,
> or cast only fast-cast spells."

A round is about six seconds. Each round Aldric gets **one action** — attack, or cast one spell,
or disengage — **plus** any fast-cast spells he can squeeze in, which are Spontaneous and
resolved out of the initiative order in response to something.

That is the whole reason the pre-combat routine matters. Every buff he casts once the swords are
out is a round he is not swinging, and his sword is very good.

**Every spell he owns casts with no roll needed and no Fatigue**, because his Casting Score alone
already exceeds the level. The margins, in the field:

| Spell | Level | Casting Total | Margin over the level |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| *Precision of the Hawk* | 10 | 29 + die | **+19** |
| *Aura of Martial Precision* | 20 | 29 + die | **+9** |
| *Gift of the Bear's Fortitude* | 25 | 29 + die | **+4** |
| *Aegis of Hardened Flesh* | 25 | 29 + die | **+4** |
| *Hide of the Whole Warband* | 25 | 29 + die | **+4** |
| *Endurance of the Berserkers* | 15 | 18 + die | **+3** |
| *Gift of the Frog's Legs* | 15 | 18 + die | **+3** |
| *Bind Wound* | 10 | 32 + die | **+22** |
| *Purification of the Festering Wounds* | 20 | 32 + die | **+12** |
| *Rally of the Fallen* | 25 | 32 + die | **+7** |

So the die never decides whether a spell works. **The only thing that can fail one is a botch** —
a zero on the stress die followed by a zero on the botch die, about 1% per cast under stress,
which also sets the Casting Total to zero and hands him a Warping Point per zero rolled on the botch dice.

> "If the maga is relaxed while casting a Formulaic spell, then there are no botch dice, no
> matter how many the circumstances would normally add."

**Buffs cast before the fighting starts cannot botch at all.** That is worth more than any of the
arithmetic above.

## Stage 1 — Before the march

Cast at the covenant, **out of armour** (Encumbrance 0) and **in the aura** (+3), while relaxed
so there are no botch dice. Both Moon-duration buffs last up to a month, so this is done once a
season, not once a battle.

| Cast on | Spell | Level | Casting Total here | Lasts |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| The whole warband, up to ten men | *Aura of Martial Precision* | 20 | 34, no botch dice | A month |
| The vanguard, individually | *Precision of the Hawk* | 10 | 34, no botch dice | A month |

**A base Corpus Group is about ten adult humans**, which is the size of warband one casting of
*Aura of Martial Precision* covers. Everyone must be together when it is cast:

> "The things in the Group when the spell is cast are affected for the entire duration, even if
> they split up. **Things that join the Group during the spell duration are not affected.**"

So muster everyone first. A grog who joins the column the next morning is unbuffed for the month.

**Warping check.** Both spells are fifth magnitude or lower, so nobody takes a Warping Point from
the power of the effect. But an effect running continuously for a month is close to the line on
constant exposure — *"a character who is constantly under the influence of one or more active
supernatural effects gains one Warping Point per year for each effect"*. One point a year for a
grog is a fair trade; four simultaneous month-long buffs, renewed all year, is not.

## Stage 2 — The hour before contact

Order matters here more than anywhere else on this page.

**1. Out of armour, cast *Gift of the Bear's Fortitude* on himself.**
Range: Personal, Duration: Sun, so it lasts to the next sunrise or sunset. Personal is the only
Range that ignores his own Magic Resistance:

> "Spells cast with Personal Range do not have to overcome the caster's own Magic Resistance.
> Spells cast with any other Range, **even if cast by the maga on herself**, do have to overcome
> the caster's own Magic Resistance."

Soak **14 → 17**.

**2. Perform the Parma Magica ritual.** Two minutes, lasts until the next sunrise or sunset.
Magic Resistance becomes Corpus 12 + (5 × Parma 1) = **17 against Corpus**, 5 against everything
else. Done *after* the self-buff, because anything not at Personal range would have to beat that
17 — and suppressing the Parma to get around it takes concentration and strips all his resistance
at once.

**3. Arm.** Load 9 → Burden 3 → Encumbrance 2. This costs him 2 Casting Score and 2 Initiative
and **nothing at all on Attack or Defense**:

> "Most athletic activities are penalized, as is spell casting, but, **in combat, Attack and
> Defense are not**, as long as the Encumbrance is largely due to weapons and armor."

There is never a reason to fight this character out of armour.

**4. Form up.** Decide the group structure before contact — it cannot be changed once engaged:

> "The leader and vanguard may only be changed while the group is not in combat."

**Aldric should not lead the group.** A trained group's coordinated size is capped by the
leader's Leadership score, and its bonus by three times it. Aldric's Leadership is 2, so he could
coordinate two men for a +6 bonus. A grog sergeant with Leadership 4 coordinates four for +12.
Give the sergeant the group and let Aldric fight beside it.

**5. Decide whether he is defended.** The Defender rules let one or two shield grogs stand for him:

> "The defended character is only injured if the defenders botch or are incapacitated."

That is the standard way a magus survives a battlefield, and Aldric's Compassionate Flaw means he
will refuse it the moment somebody else is bleeding. Name that tension before the dice come out
rather than during.

## Stage 3 — Initiative

Rolled **once**, at the start, and it stands for the whole combat.

He has two Initiative Totals and must decide which he is rolling *before* he knows what the round
holds — spells carry no weapon Initiative modifier, but Fast Caster adds three:

| | Total | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Attacking with the sword | **+1 + die** | Qik 1 + Init 2 − Enc 2 |
| Casting a spell | **+2 + die** | Qik 1 − Enc 2 + Fast Caster 3 |

**Casting is his faster action.** That is unusual and worth remembering: if the first round is
going to be a buff, he acts earlier than if he were swinging.

He may also hold: *"a party with a high initiative may delay its action to respond to a later
action in the round."*

## Stage 4 — Round by round

Work down this list and take the first line that applies.

### 1. Is anyone about to be hit hard who is not Aldric? → *Aegis of Hardened Flesh*

MuCo 25, Touch, Diameter — **twenty combat rounds**, so it covers the whole melee. +3 Soak.

This is the highest-value action he has, and the numbers are not close. Against a trained
man-at-arms attacking with a longsword (Attack +9, damage +6):

| Target | Soak | Wounded per attack | With *Aegis* (+3 Soak) | Risk removed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A grog, Size 0, Defense +8 | 7 | **45.2%** | 23.4% | **21.8 points** |
| Aldric, Size +1, Defense +12 | 14 | **3.8%** | 2.3% | 1.5 points |

The same spell is worth **fourteen times more** on a grog than on himself. Aldric is already
nearly untouchable by ordinary soldiers; his men are not. Cast it outward.

### 2. Is the line about to receive a charge? → *Hide of the Whole Warband*

MuCo 25, Touch, Diameter, Group. +1 Soak to everyone, up to ten men. Worth 7.7 points of wound
risk per grog per attack — less per man than *Aegis*, far more in total across a line of six.

### 3. Is somebody down and bleeding? → *Bind Wound*, then get back in the line

CrCo 10, Touch, Sun. Casting Total 32 against level 10. Wounds cannot worsen; no natural healing
while it runs. This is the Compassionate Flaw's action, and it is a round he is not fighting —
which is the point of the Flaw.

### 4. Otherwise: **swing the sword.**

Attack **13 + die** against an armoured soldier's Defense, damage **7 + Attack Advantage**.
Against a Size 0 soldier with Defense +8 and Soak 7:

| | Wounds on | Typical result |
|---|---:|---|
| Normal attack, Attack +13 | **82%** of attacks | Light 37%, Medium 32%, Heavy 9% |
| Mounted on the destrier, Attack +15 | **90%** | Medium 39%, Light 28%, Heavy 17% |
| Exertion, Attack +20 | **96%** | Heavy 39%, Medium 28%, Incapacitating 17% |

He is a very good soldier. Four rounds in five he wounds his man.

### 5. When he is finally in trouble: *Endurance of the Berserkers* — and then stop casting

ReCo 15, Personal, Concentration. His body acts as unwounded and unfatigued for as long as he
concentrates. Two costs, both severe:

**It does not stop the wounds.** *"A character under the influence of this spell does risk
worsening wounds, but he may not notice."* And every Fatigue level he spends while it runs falls
due the instant it ends.

**It shuts down his magic.** Casting while maintaining a Concentration spell is Ease Factor 15,
and that row is not reduced for maintenance. His Concentration roll is Sta 2 + Concentration 2 =
4 + a stress die, so he needs an 11 or better on the die: **5.9%.** Treat *Endurance of the
Berserkers* as the button he presses when the buffing is finished and he intends to do nothing
but fight.

### Reactions, out of the initiative order

**Exertion** — one Fatigue level for +7. On attack it applies to a single roll; on Defense it
applies to *every* Defense roll until his turn comes round again, which usually makes it the
better buy. Defense **12 → 19**.

**Fast-cast** — Qik 1 + Finesse 1 + Fast Caster 3 = **5 + die** against the opponent's Initiative
Total. Fast-cast spells are Spontaneous unless he has mastered the spell, at −10 to the Casting
Score and +2 botch dice. His best fast-cast is a fatiguing Spontaneous Creo Corpus effect:
(41 − 10 + die) ÷ 2, so roughly **level 18** on an average die. Enough to improvise a heal or a
shove; not enough to be a plan.

## Target numbers he will actually roll

Concentration is **4 + a stress die**. Maintenance rows have the standard Ease Factor reduced by
three; the second table is used as printed.

| Situation | Ease Factor | Needs on the die | Chance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Maintaining, standing still | −3 | automatic | 100% |
| Maintaining, walking | 0 | automatic | 100% |
| Maintaining, jostled or running | 6 | 2+ | **90%** |
| Maintaining, dodging or knocked down | 9 | 5+ | **59%** |
| Maintaining, damaged this round | 12 | 8+ | **28%** |
| Casting while maintaining another spell | 15 | 11+ | **5.9%** |
| Casting, walking | 3 | automatic | 100% |
| Casting, jostled or running | 9 | 5+ | **59%** |
| Casting, knocked down | 12 | 8+ | **28%** |
| Casting, damaged this round | 15 | 11+ | **5.9%** |

Failing a concentration roll fails the spell *and* adds a botch die to the casting roll.

**Read the bottom half of that table again.** His spells never fail on their Casting Totals, but
casting in the middle of a melee fails on *Concentration* better than a third of the time, and
after he has been hit it is hopeless. Aldric's magic belongs in the minute before contact and in
the gaps, not in the press. Concentration 2 is the number holding this character back, and
raising it is the cheapest improvement available to him.

**Stress die reference**, since so much here turns on it — 1 rerolls and doubles, 0 is zero and
may botch:

| Need | 2+ | 3+ | 5+ | 6+ | 8+ | 9+ | 11+ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Chance | 90% | 80% | 59% | 49% | 28% | 17% | 5.9% |

## What the buffing is actually worth

The argument for this character, in one table. A trained group of six grogs — vanguard damage +6,
enemy Defense +8 and Soak 7 — with the group inflicting its damage once per member:

| *Aura of Martial Precision* | Damage per blow | × 6 men | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Not cast | 2.22 | **13.3 per round** | — |
| +1 Attack | 2.77 | **16.6 per round** | **+25%** |
| +2 Attack (if the troupe allows the stronger reading) | 3.39 | **20.3 per round** | +53% |

The mechanism is that **Attack Advantage is added to the Damage Total**, so a point of Attack is
also a point of damage on every blow that lands, multiplied by the number of men in the group.
One level-20 spell, cast once a month, is worth about a quarter of the warband's output.

## Traps

**He cannot buff a fellow magus without Penetration.** Penetration Total = Casting Total +
Penetration Bonus − spell level. *Aura of Martial Precision* on a sodalis: 29 + 2 − 20 = **11 +
die**, against their Corpus score plus five times their Parma. Against a typical magus that fails.
Grogs and mundane soldiers have no Magic Resistance at all and are always affected.

**Nothing he casts on another person may exceed level 25.** Level 26 is sixth magnitude, which
makes a spell a powerful supernatural effect, and everyone subjected to one takes a Warping
Point. A buff-mage who ignores this Warps his own warband.

**Vis is a bad idea in a fight.** Each pawn adds +2 to the Casting Score and **+1 botch die**. He
does not need the +2 — every spell already carries — so the only thing vis buys him in combat is
the chance of a disaster.

**A botched Defense roll sets the total to zero**, handing the attacker an Attack Advantage equal
to their whole Attack Total. It is roughly one attack in a hundred, and it is very nearly the
only way an ordinary soldier kills him. It is also the argument for shield grogs and for spending
Fatigue on Exertion when the odds get long.

**Wound penalties never apply to Soak** — *"Soak is not an action"* — so a badly wounded Aldric is
exactly as hard to hurt as a fresh one. What collapses is his Attack, his Defense and his casting.

**Settle the gestures question before play.** The rules impose no free-hand requirement for bold
gestures — the only "free hands" clauses in combat cover grappling and bows — so by the book he
casts normally with sword and shield in hand. If your storyguide rules otherwise, he loses 5 for
casting without gestures *and* the 3 from Method Caster, since that Virtue requires he not vary
his method: Muto Corpus drops from 29 to **21**, which still carries *Aura of Martial Precision*
at 20 but falls four short of the level-25 buffs, costing a Fatigue level each time.

## Worked example — a skirmish on the road

**The warband.** Aldric, plus four grogs as a trained group: sergeant Bertrand as leader and
vanguard, Attack +9, Defense +8, Damage +6, Soak 7, Single Weapon 4, Leadership 4. The group's
bonus is the other three men's Combat Abilities, 4 + 4 + 4 = 12, capped at three times Bertrand's
Leadership, also 12 — so **+12 to Attack or Defense, chosen fresh each round**.

**The enemy.** Eight brigands in two untrained groups of four. Attack +7, Defense +6, Damage +5,
Soak 5, Size 0, Initiative +2.

**Already running,** from the muster a fortnight ago: *Aura of Martial Precision* on all five, so
Bertrand attacks at +10. And from this morning: *Gift of the Bear's Fortitude*, Aldric at Soak
17, with his Parma up.

**Initiative**, rolled once and standing for the whole fight. Bertrand's group and both brigand
groups have Initiative +2. Aldric intends to open with a spell, so he uses his casting Initiative
of +2 rather than his sword's +1 — Fast Caster more than covers the missing weapon modifier.

| | Modifier | Die | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Brigands B | +2 | 8 | **10** |
| Bertrand's group | +2 | 6 | **8** |
| Aldric | +2 | 5 | **7** |
| Brigands A | +2 | 3 | **5** |

**Round 1.** Brigands B close first but reach nobody. Bertrand's group puts its +12 into Attack
and goes into group A. Aldric, on 7, casts *Hide of the Whole Warband* — Casting Total 29 against
level 25, so the die is irrelevant — and every man in his line goes to Soak 8 for the next twenty
rounds. Their chance of being wounded by any given brigand blow falls from 45% to 37%.

**Round 2.** Group B pushes towards Aldric. He casts *Aegis of Hardened Flesh* on Bertrand:
Soak 8 → **11**, which cuts the sergeant's chance of being wounded from 37% to **17.8%** for the
rest of the fight. He is jostled as he casts, so a Concentration roll against Ease Factor 9 —
4 + 6 = 10, which holds.

**Round 3.** Buffing done, sword out. Attack 13, rolls 7, total 20 against a brigand's Defense
6 + 4 = 10. Attack Advantage 10, Damage 7 + 10 = 17, less Soak 5 = **12 over**. At Size 0 the
bands run Light 1–5, Medium 6–10, Heavy 11–15, so that is a **Heavy Wound** and the brigand is at
−5 to everything.

**Round 4.** Three brigands reach him. Two miss against Defense 12 + die. The third rolls
7 for Attack 14; Aldric rolls 3 for Defense 15 — no advantage, no hit. He does not spend Fatigue;
at Soak 17 against damage +5 a brigand needs an Attack Advantage of 13 to raise a bruise, and he
would rather keep the Fatigue levels.

**Round 5.** A brigand rolls a 1, exploding to 16, for Attack 23; Aldric rolls a 0, checks his
botch die, gets a 4 and is safe, so his Defense is 12. Attack Advantage 11, damage 5 + 11 = 16
against Soak 17 — nothing. **This is why the Soak buff was cast at dawn.**

**Round 6.** Aldric declares Exertion, spending a Fatigue level to Winded — no penalty at that
level — for Attack **20 + die**. He rolls 6: total 26 against Defense 11. Attack Advantage 15,
damage 22, less Soak 5 = 17 over. At Size 0 that is an **Incapacitating Wound**. The brigand
drops and the rest break.

**Afterwards.** *Rally of the Fallen* over the wounded — CrCo 25, Touch, Moon, Group, Casting
Total 32, no focus because healing falls outside it — giving every man +6 to Recovery rolls for a
month, provided the spell covers the whole recovery interval. Then *Bind Wound* on anyone who
cannot be moved.

Aldric cast four spells in the whole engagement, three of them on other people, and spent three
of his six rounds with a sword in his hand. That is the shape of a good fight for this character.
